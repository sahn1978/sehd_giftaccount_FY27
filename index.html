How the equivalency score is calculated

The headline percentage blends independent signals so that no single measure dominates. Each signal is scored from 0 to 1 and then combined with the weights below.

Semantic meaning (weight 0.45 when enabled)

A sentence-embedding model (all-MiniLM-L6-v2, run locally in your browser through Transformers.js) converts the incoming description and every UM description into vectors, then measures cosine similarity. This captures meaning even when the two catalogs use different wording.

Term frequency similarity, TF-IDF (weight 0.25)

Both descriptions are turned into weighted keyword vectors where rare, informative words count more than common ones. Cosine similarity of these vectors rewards genuine content overlap.

Key-topic overlap (weight 0.15)

The share of distinctive content words the two courses hold in common, so strongly overlapping topics rank higher.

Title similarity (weight 0.15)

Direct overlap between the two course titles, a high-signal cue when titles are close.

When semantic matching is off or still loading, the remaining three signals are re-weighted to sum to one, so the tool stays fully usable with no model. Credit-hour and course-level differences are shown as advisory flags and lightly adjust the final number rather than driving it, because a course can be equivalent in content while differing in credits.

Bands: Strong 70 and above, Probable 50 to 69, Possible 32 to 49, Weak below 32. Treat them as a starting point for review.
